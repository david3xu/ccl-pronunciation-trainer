# DI Template Design Rules

## What Makes a Good DI Template?

### Core Principles

1. **Easy to Fill**
   - Clear placeholders that are obvious what information goes where
   - Minimal thinking required during the exam
   - Structured sections that guide you step-by-step

2. **Helps Keep Fluency**
   - Natural transitions between sentences
   - Connecting phrases that maintain smooth speech flow
   - No awkward pauses while thinking of what to say next
   - Rhythm and pacing that sounds conversational

3. **Easy to Speak**
   - Simple, pronounceable words
   - Natural English phrases (not overly academic or complex)
   - Avoids tongue-twisters or difficult consonant clusters
   - Words that flow naturally in spoken English

### Template Structure

A good template should have:
- **Opening** - Identify the image type and topic
- **Body** - Describe key data/features systematically
- **Conclusion** - Brief summary statement

### Critical Rule: Direct Reading from Image

**All filled words should come directly from the image itself**
- Read labels, titles, axis names, legends EXACTLY as shown
- Minimize interpretation or paraphrasing
- Use the exact terminology from the graph/chart/table
- This reduces cognitive load and speeds up response time
- Ensures accuracy and avoids mistakes

### Balance Required

- **Formal enough** for academic exam context (PTE)
- **Simple enough** to pronounce confidently under pressure
- **Structured enough** to guide you without memorization
- **Flexible enough** to adapt to different images

### What to Avoid

- Overly complex vocabulary that's hard to pronounce
- Too generic/vague descriptions that sound childish
- Too rigid structure that doesn't fit all image types
- Sentences that are too long and risk losing fluency
