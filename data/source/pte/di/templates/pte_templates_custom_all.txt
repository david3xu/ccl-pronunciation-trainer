# PTE Academic Test Templates - Speaking & Writing

## Usage Guidelines
- Speak naturally with proper rhythm, pausing, and intonation
- Avoid mechanical recitation - sound conversational
- Fill templates with content from the actual test material
- Prioritize pronunciation ease over complexity

---

## 1. DESCRIBE IMAGE Templates

### Template A: Data Charts - Single Category (Bar/Line/Pie/Table/Map)
**Transition Style: Comparison Flow**

```
This [chart type] shows information about [title].

At the top, [largest item] has the highest number, which is around [max value].

Coming in next, [second item] shows about [second value].

On the other end, [smallest item] has the lowest at around [min value].

Worth noting, both [other item 1] and [other item 2] carry weight here too.

In summary, this chart presents real data about [title].
```

### Template B: Data Charts - Multiple Categories
**Transition Style: Category Switching**

```
This [chart type] gives information about [title].

When we check [category 1], [cat1 max item] leads at around [cat1 max value], while [cat1 min item] sits lowest at about [cat1 min value].

Switching to [category 2], [cat2 max item] tops this group at around [cat2 max value].

As for other data, [any item 1] and [any item 2] also play a role in this chart.

Overall, the data compares different groups about [title].
```

### Template C: Flow Charts/Process Diagrams
**Transition Style: Sequential Movement**

```
This flow chart shows the process of [title].

Everything begins with [step 1] as the starting point.

Moving forward, the next stage involves [step 2].

Continuing on, [step 3] comes into play.

To wrap things up, [last step] marks the final stage.

In summary, these steps show how [title] works.
```

*For循环图: Replace last sentence with: "Once finished, everything loops back to the beginning."*

### Template D: General Images/Photos
**Transition Style: Spatial Exploration**

```
This picture shows information about [title].

Right in [area 1/position 1], ___ stands out as the main focus.

Just beside that, ___ and ___ add important context.

If we shift to [area 2/position 2], ___ and ___ bring in more details.

Scattered around, ___ and ___ fill out the scene as well.

Overall, these elements work together to present [title].
```

### Template E: Maps/Geographic Images
**Transition Style: Directional Guide**

```
This map gives details about [title].

Starting from the top, [location 1] appears with [detail].

Heading to the center, [location 2] shows up with [detail].

Down at the bottom, [location 3] sits at around [detail].

Looking at key spots, [feature 1] and [feature 2] mark important locations.

In total, this map displays the layout of [title].
```

## 2. RETELL LECTURE Template

```
This lecture mainly discusses [topic].

First, the lecture starts by introducing [point] and [point].

Second, [point] and [point] are mentioned, which connects to the topic.

Third, the speaker gives many details about [point] and [point].

Also, the lecture discusses [point] and [point] to support the main idea.

Overall, the lecture covers the topic of [topic].
```

**Tips:**
- Note down "phrases" not just single words
- If you have complete sentences, paraphrase them
- Each content sentence has 2 blanks - combine points with "and" if needed
- Repeat points if you don't have enough content

## 4. RESPOND TO A SITUATION Template

### Opening (1-2 sentences)
**Known recipient:** Hi / Excuse me, [name].
**Unknown recipient:** Hi, excuse me! / Hi, how are you?
**Phone message:** Hi, this is [your name].

*For formal contexts:* Hope I'm not interrupting! / May I have a moment?

### Body (2-3 sentences)
Paraphrase the situation in first person, add relevant details

### Closing (1-2 sentences)

**Making suggestions:**
- Informal: I think it's a good idea if you/we can...
- Formal: I suggest we consider...

**Seeking advice:**
- Informal: What do you think about...?
- Formal: Could you provide some guidance on...?

**Seeking help:**
- Informal: Would you help me with...? Thanks so much!
- Formal: Would it be possible for you/me to...? I'd appreciate your help.

**Apologizing:**
- Informal: Sorry about that!
- Formal: I sincerely apologize for...

**Thanking:**
- Informal: Thank you for...!
- Formal: I'm deeply grateful for your...

## 5. SUMMARIZE SPOKEN TEXT Template (50-70 words)

### Full Template (28 words base)

```
The lecture discusses the topic of [topic]. First, the speaker mentioned that [key point]. Second, the lecturer showed that [key point]. In addition/However, the speaker emphasized that [key point]. Moreover, the talk highlighted that [key point].
```

### Simplified Template (12 words base)
*For students who can capture more content*

```
The lecture discusses the topic of [topic]. First, [key point]. Second, [key point]. In addition/However, [key point]. Moreover, [key point].
```

**Note:** Must be written as one continuous paragraph (no line breaks)

## 6. SUMMARIZE WRITTEN TEXT Template (5-75 words)

```
______, [connector] _____, [connector] ______, [connector] ______, [connector] ______.
```

### Common Connectors:
- **Parallel:** and
- **Contrast:** but, whereas
- **Concession:** although
- **Cause:** because
- **Result:** so
- **Accompanying:** while
- **Explanation:** which means

**Tips:**
- Select key sentences from each section
- Focus on first/last sentences and logical markers
- Paraphrase the original text
- Use commas and connectors (only ONE period at the very end)
- Must be one complete sentence within 5-75 words

## 7. WRITE ESSAY Templates (200-300 words)

### Option A: Original Template (151 words base) - Safe for 65-79 score

**Paragraph 1**
```
There is no doubt that the debate over [keywords] has drawn attention from the general public in the modern world.

Personally speaking, I firmly believe that [your position], which will be supported by the following discussion.
```

**Paragraph 2**
```
First and foremost, when it comes to [keywords], the most important [reason/advantage/feature] is that [keywords] can potentially bring about [keywords].

To be more specific, [keywords] will generate [keywords], which will consequently result in [keywords].

For instance, according to the annual report of [source 1], at least 80% of [relevant subjects] have experienced [keywords] because of [keywords].
```

**Paragraph 3**
```
On the other hand, in terms of [keywords], another/one significant [reason/disadvantage/solution/feature] is that [keywords] can possibly cause [keywords].

To illustrate, [keywords] can give rise to [keywords], thereby leading to [keywords].

To take a recent study from [source 2] as an example, more than 60% of [relevant subjects] have faced [keywords] as a result of [keywords].
```

**Paragraph 4**
```
With all the points above, it can be concluded that [rephrase position], because [keywords] and [keywords] both play a vital role in this case.

Therefore, it is of utmost importance that [relevant subjects] should devote [resources/time/efforts/energy/money] to this.
```

### Option B: 90+ Optimized Template (155 words base) - For Advanced Scores

**Paragraph 1**
```
In recent years, the topic of [keywords] has emerged as a prominent subject of public and academic discussion.

I contend that [your position], and the following analysis presents the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.
```

**Paragraph 2**
```
To begin with, one compelling [reason/advantage/feature] of [keywords] is its potential to facilitate [keywords].

In particular, [keywords] can foster [keywords], ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as [keywords].

For instance, evidence from [source 1] reveals that approximately 80% of [relevant subjects] experienced [keywords] as a direct result of [keywords].
```

**Paragraph 3**
```
However, it is equally important to acknowledge that [keywords] may also give rise to certain challenges.

This is largely because [keywords] are likely to generate [keywords], which may subsequently cause [keywords].

According to research by [source 2], over 60% of [relevant subjects] have been affected by [keywords] stemming from [keywords].
```

**Paragraph 4**
```
Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that [rephrase position], as the influence of [keywords] and [keywords] is substantial and far-reaching.

Thus, it is imperative for [relevant subjects] to dedicate adequate [resources/time/efforts/energy/money] to managing this issue effectively.
```

**Why Option B scores 90+:**
- ✅ Advanced vocabulary (contend, facilitate, foster, imperative, substantial)
- ✅ Complex grammar structures correctly used
- ✅ "However" connector works for ALL essay types (universal)
- ✅ Clear logical flow: advantage → disadvantage structure
- ✅ Academic C1-C2 level writing
- ✅ All 7 PTE criteria optimized

## Key Reminders for Speaking Sections

1. **Natural delivery is crucial** - avoid robotic memorization
2. **Easy pronunciation** - choose words you can say confidently
3. **Logical flow** - ensure your content makes sense
4. **Time management** - fill templates appropriately for the time limit
5. **First-person perspective** - especially for "Respond to a Situation"

## Key Reminders for Writing Sections

1. **Grammar accuracy** - simple sentences are fine
2. **Word count** - always verify you're within limits
3. **Paraphrasing** - don't copy directly from prompts
4. **Logical connectors** - use appropriate linking words
5. **Complete sentences** - especially for SWT (one sentence only)
