# 🎯 DI Templates - Fluency Optimized (2025)

**Design Philosophy:**
- ✅ Natural speech rhythm (8-12 words per phrase)
- ✅ Academic vocabulary that's easy to pronounce
- ✅ Fewer pauses = better oral fluency
- ✅ Works with bracket-based parser `[variable content]`

---

## Template A: Line/Bar Charts - Single Category Trend

**Use for:** Line graphs, bar charts showing one variable over time/categories

```
This [LINE graph / BAR chart / PIE chart] ILLUSTRATES information REGARDING [topic from title].

At the TOP [highest item] SHOWS the HIGHEST value |
of around [max value with units].

Following THAT [second item] SHOWS about [second value] |
representing the SECOND-highest figure.

On the other END [lowest item] SHOWS the LOWEST value |
at around [min value with units].

Worth noting [other item 1] and [other item 2] |
also contribute to the data ANALYSIS.

In CONCLUSION this chart PRESENTS comprehensive DATA |
demonstrating CLEAR variations in [topic from title].
```

**Key Features:**
- "ILLUSTRATES" / "SHOWS" / "PRESENTS" (easy academic verbs)
- "around" / "about" (not "approximately")
- "Following THAT" (not "Following this")
- "On the other END" (not "Conversely")
- "Worth noting" (natural transition)

## Template B: Multiple Categories Comparison

**Use for:** Charts comparing 2-3 groups (bar charts, line graphs with multiple series)

```
This [chart type] ILLUSTRATES information REGARDING [topic from title].

Looking at [category 1] [cat1 max item] SHOWS the HIGHEST value |
at around [cat1 max value] |
while [cat1 min item] SHOWS the LOWEST at about [cat1 min value].

Switching to [category 2] [cat2 max item] LEADS this group |
at around [cat2 max value] |
followed by [cat2 second item] at about [cat2 second value].

Worth noting [any other item 1] and [any other item 2] |
also play a role in this DATA.

In CONCLUSION this chart PRESENTS |
the COMPARATIVE analysis between [the categories being compared].
```

**Key Features:**
- "Looking at" / "Switching to" (natural category transitions)
- "LEADS this group" (easier than "demonstrates the highest")
- "followed by" (natural flow)
- All-caps for stress words only (not entire phrases)

## Template C: Process Charts / Flow Diagrams

**Use for:** Process diagrams, cycle charts, sequential flows

```
This FLOW chart SHOWS the process of [topic from title].

Everything STARTS with [step 1] as the FIRST stage.

Moving FORWARD [step 2] comes into play |
followed by [step 3].

After THAT [step 4] takes place |
leading to [step 5].

At the END [final step] marks the FINAL stage.

In CONCLUSION these steps SHOW |
how [topic from title] works.
```

**For cycle diagrams:**
Replace last sentence with:
```
Once COMPLETE the process LOOPS back to the beginning.
```

## Template D: General Images / Photos

**Use for:** Pictures, photos, illustrations without numerical data

```
This IMAGE SHOWS information about [topic from title].

Right in [area 1 / foreground / center] [main element] stands out |
as the MAIN focus.

Just beside THAT [element 2] and [element 3] |
add IMPORTANT context.

If we shift to [area 2 / background / side] [element 4] and [element 5] |
bring in more DETAILS.

Worth noting [element 6] and [element 7] |
fill out the scene as WELL.

In CONCLUSION these elements work TOGETHER |
to present [topic from title].
```

## Template E: Maps / Geographic Images

**Use for:** Maps, geographic layouts, spatial data

```
This MAP SHOWS details about [topic from title].

Starting from the TOP [location 1] appears with [detail].

Moving to the CENTER [location 2] shows up with [detail].

Down at the BOTTOM [location 3] sits at around [detail].

Looking at key SPOTS [feature 1] and [feature 2] |
mark IMPORTANT locations.

In CONCLUSION this map DISPLAYS |
the layout of [topic from title].
```

## 📝 Vocabulary Choices Explained

### ✅ **Verbs We Use (Easy + Academic):**
- ILLUSTRATES (shows relationship)
- SHOWS (universal, easy)
- PRESENTS (formal, easy)
- DEMONSTRATES (selective use only)
- INDICATES (for pointing out)

### ❌ **Verbs We Avoid (Too Formal/Hard):**
- ~~Elucidates~~
- ~~Delineates~~
- ~~Exemplifies~~

### ✅ **Transitions We Use (Natural):**
- Following THAT (not "Following this")
- Worth noting (not "Additionally")
- On the other END (not "Conversely")
- Switching to (for categories)
- Moving FORWARD (for sequences)

### ✅ **Quantities We Use (Easy):**
- around / about (not "approximately")
- at / of (simple prepositions)

---

## 🎯 Fluency Rules

### 1. **Phrase Length: 8-12 words**
- ✅ "At the TOP [item] SHOWS the HIGHEST value of around [value]"
- ❌ "At the TOP, | [item] | DEMONSTRATES | the HIGHEST value, | which is approximately [value]"

### 2. **Pipe `|` = Natural Breath Point**
- Use only at sentence boundaries or natural pauses
- NOT after every 3-4 words

### 3. **Stress Words = ALL CAPS**
- Content words for emphasis (TOP, HIGHEST, SHOWS)
- NOT entire phrases

### 4. **Brackets = Variable Content**
- `[LINE graph]` = fill from image
- Parser recognizes this automatically

---

## 📊 Before/After Example

### ❌ **Before (Choppy):**
```
At the TOP, | [in SEVENTEEN hundred] | DEMONSTRATES the HIGHEST value, |
which is approximately [FIVE hundred and FORTY-four years].
```
**Problems:**
- 4 tiny segments
- "approximately" is a mouthful
- Sounds robotic

### ✅ **After (Fluent):**
```
At the TOP [in SEVENTEEN hundred] SHOWS the HIGHEST value |
of around [FIVE hundred and FORTY-four years].
```
**Improvements:**
- 2 natural phrases
- "around" flows easily
- Sounds conversational yet academic

---

## 🎤 Pronunciation Tips

1. **Don't pause at every pipe** - treat `|` as optional breath points
2. **Link words together** - "At the TOP" = "AttheTOP" in natural speech
3. **Stress the ALL CAPS** - but don't shout, just emphasize
4. **Variable content [in brackets]** - speak these clearly but naturally
5. **Practice full answers** - not individual segments

## 🔴 Watch Out: Hard Words Alert!

**Only 6 difficult words** in these templates - master these and you're golden! 🎯

### 1. **ILLUSTRATES** (IL-uh-strayts)
- ❌ Common mistake: "IL-LUST-rates" (3 syllables too clear)
- ✅ Correct: IL-uh-strayts (middle syllable very short)
- **Practice:** Say "IL-uh-strayts information REGARDING" 5 times fast

### 2. **comprehensive** (kahm-prih-HEN-siv)
- ❌ Common mistake: "COM-preh-HEN-sive" (stress on COM)
- ✅ Correct: kahm-prih-HEN-siv (stress on HEN, first syllable reduced)
- **Practice:** "comprehensive DATA" - feel the stress on HEN, not COM

### 3. **demonstrating** (DEM-uhn-stray-ting)
- ❌ Common mistake: "DEM-on-STRAY-ting" (middle syllable too clear)
- ✅ Correct: DEM-uhn-stray-ting (say it fast, middle almost disappears)
- **Practice:** "DEM-un-stray-ting CLEAR variations" - speed it up!

### 4. **representing** (rep-rih-ZEN-ting)
- ❌ Common mistake: "REP-re-sent-ing" (stress on first)
- ✅ Correct: rep-rih-ZEN-ting (stress on ZEN!)
- **Practice:** "rep-rih-ZEN-ting the SECOND-highest figure"

### 5. **COMPARATIVE** (kuhm-PAIR-uh-tiv)
- ❌ Common mistake: "COM-par-a-tive" (first syllable too strong)
- ✅ Correct: kuhm-PAIR-uh-tiv (first syllable weak: "kuhm")
- **Practice:** "the kuhm-PAIR-uh-tiv analysis between"

### 6. **variations** (vair-ee-AY-shuhnz)
- ❌ Common mistake: "VAIR-ee-ay-shunz" (stress on first)
- ✅ Correct: vair-ee-AY-shuhnz (stress on AY!)
- **Practice:** Think "vary" → "vari-AY-shun" → fast: "vair-ee-AY-shuhnz"

**💡 Quick Drill:**
- Record yourself saying these 6 words
- Compare with Google Translate or TTS
- Practice them in template phrases (see examples above)
- **That's it!** These are the ONLY tricky words - everything else is easy! 🎉

**Pro Tip:** If you mess up one of these during the test, keep going! Fluency matters more than perfect pronunciation of every single syllable.
